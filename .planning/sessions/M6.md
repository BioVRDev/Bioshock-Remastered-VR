# M6 — the hand rig: separate hands, two-handed grip, left-handed mode

Opened 2026-08-10, after M7 closed. Researched in
**`docs/proposals/vr-features-research.md`**, which found the thing that shapes
this whole milestone:

> **Three requested features are one mechanism.** Left-handed mode, independently
> tracked hands and a two-handed grip are all *a rigid transform applied to one
> bone cluster*. Build it once and the three become configuration.
> `docs/modules/hands.md` § *Future direction* described this mechanism before
> any of them were asked for.

Shared context: `CLAUDE.md` · `docs/modules/hands.md` ·
`docs/ENGINE-MAP.md` § *Hands actor* and § *Skeleton* ·
`docs/proposals/vr-features-research.md` · **`docs/INVARIANTS.md` § *Settled***,
specifically the dirty-byte entry — it is the trap this milestone will hit.

---

## What is already known, so no session re-derives it

`Hands/ArmHide.cpp` has the whole rig mapped and locked, fail-closed:

```
AHands           +0x3FC -> SkeletonInstance*
SkeletonInstance +0x48  -> hkQsTransform* render bone array
SkeletonInstance +0x4C  -> bone count, exactly 47
SkeletonInstance +0x88  -> evaluate-if-dirty byte
```

`hkQsTransform` is 48 bytes — `position[4]`, **`rotation[4]` (quaternion)**,
`scale[4]`. `ArmHide` writes position and scale today; **the rotation lane is
untouched and is the whole of what this milestone needs.**

| Cluster | Bones | Wrist | Sleeve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 6–21 | 6 | 3, 4, 5, 22, 23 |
| Right | 27–44 | 27 | 24, 25, 26, 45, 46 |

**Bone 43 is the weapon attachment** and must never be scaled — the attachment
path inverse-decomposes bone scale, so a zero there divides by zero and throws
the weapon across the near plane.

---

## ⚠ The trap this milestone will hit

**Writing bones freezes the bone array.** `ArmHide` clears the dirty byte so its
writes stick, which stops the engine re-evaluating the array at all. M7-S4 hit
this and produced a bistable latch that took a headset cycle to diagnose —
`docs/INVARIANTS.md`, *you cannot hide by bone and measure by bone at the same
time*.

**M7 now depends on that array staying live.** `ArmHide_HandMotion` samples bone
27 to decide whether the arms show during a scripted sequence, and that feature
is signed off and working. A cluster write that clears the dirty byte will break
it silently.

So every session here must answer: *does this write run while the motion signal
is being trusted?* The clean separation is that cluster writes belong to
ordinary 6-DOF play and stand down during scripted sequences, where the game
owns the hands anyway.

## M6-S1 · Prove the space, then move one cluster

**Status:** ✅ **DONE, verified in the headset 2026-08-10.** Tester: "Looks and
feels fantastic. Works perfectly, even in the scripted events." · Four cycles,
not the estimated two — the extra two were a palm artefact and a hand-coupling
bug, both found in the headset and both fixed.

### What shipped

A **tracked left hand**, `OffHandTracked` in the ini, default off. It appears and
follows your own controller on exactly the weapons that hide it today; the
two-handed weapons are untouched. Live numpad tuning for position and rotation,
saved back to the ini.

### What it measured, and what that killed

- **The array is a common model space, centimetres, on the actor's axes.**
  Confirmed, not inferred. `docs/ENGINE-MAP.md` § *Skeleton*.
- **The array stays live in ordinary play with the dirty byte cleared.** The
  worry that a cluster write would freeze the gun hand was unfounded — bone 27
  moved between every pair of dumps. Scoped to ordinary play only; M7-S4's latch
  was real and is a different window.
- **The left cluster is not animated at all**, so a rigid transform on it fights
  nothing.
- **"Apply late" was wrong for bones.** S59/S60 measured the *actor rotator*
  being erased, not the bone array. CalcView + the dirty byte is the seam.

### The two bugs the headset found, both instructive

1. **A sharp spike out of the palm.** Vertices weighted across a bone we moved
   and a bone we did not: `CollapseArm` pins the sleeve at the wrist, but runs
   before the cluster write and reads the *engine's* wrist. Diagnosed from one
   tester observation — the spike followed the right hand while the rest of the
   hand followed the left.
2. **The right hand dragged the left.** The placement offset was applied in the
   actor's frame, and the actor is rotated by the weapon hand. Everything else
   cancels the actor out algebraically, so the offset was the only term that
   *could* couple them.

### Also found

`dllmain build:` does not always advance — `__TIME__` is baked in when
`dllmain.cpp` compiles, so an incremental build that misses that file ships a new
DLL reporting an old time. Recorded in `docs/INVARIANTS.md`. Bump the label every
session.

Numpad 9 cycled the tuning mode **and** dumped a frame's render-target structure
— ~400 lines to disk per press, 2012 in one measured session. The dump moved
to F3.

### The one idea
Apply a rigid transform to the **off-hand** cluster so it tracks its own
controller, independently of the actor transform that carries the weapon hand.

### Prove this before building on it
The design assumes the render bone array is in a **common (model) space**, not
parent-relative. That is *inferred*, not measured — the evidence is that
`CollapseBone(idx, wrist.position)` pins sleeve bones at the **wrist's**
position, which is meaningless in a parent-relative array, and that
`kFarBelow = {0,0,-5000}` works the same way.

**Measure it first:** log three bones of one cluster at rest and check the
positions differ by plausible anatomical distances in a shared frame. One log
line, and it gates everything below. If it is parent-relative instead, the whole
transform maths changes and it is better to know in the first ten minutes.

### The mechanism
Capture a reference pose once. Each frame choose a target transform for the
cluster's wrist and rewrite every bone in the cluster as
`target x (reference_wrist⁻¹ x reference_bone)`. Clear the dirty byte. Apply it
**late** — S59/S60 measured the game tick erasing hand *roll* every single frame,
which is why `CameraHook_LateHandsWrite` re-applies from Present. A cluster write
from `CalcView` will be erased the same way.

### Specification
1. **Off-hand only.** The weapon hand keeps riding the actor transform, which
   works and is calibrated per weapon slot.
2. **Default off**, new INI switch.
3. **Stand down during scripted sequences** — see the trap above.
4. **Fail closed** on the same guards `ArmHide` already uses: vtables, skeleton
   ownership, exactly 47 bones. A wrong offset here produces a bone matrix full
   of garbage, not a glitch.
5. **Test on slots 2 and 5** (shotgun, machine gun). `HideInactiveHand2=0` and
   `HideInactiveHand5=0` already exist so those weapons keep both hands, which
   makes them the natural first cases.

### Falsifiable outcome
The off-hand follows its controller while the weapon stays in the other hand,
and scripted-sequence arm behaviour is unchanged.

## M6-S2 · Two-handed grip
**Depends on:** S1.

The same write, different target: the off-hand cluster tracks **a point on the
weapon** rather than the controller — a per-slot offset tuned exactly the way
`gripSlot`/`rotSlot` already are, with the live-numpad-and-save workflow.

Engage when the off-hand controller is physically near that point and the grip is
held; release when either fails. **The game has no concept of a second hand on a
weapon** — nothing in the corpus suggests one — so this is purely a mod-side
visual and cannot affect recoil or accuracy without further work.

## M6-S3 · Left-handed mode
**Depends on:** S1.

**Buy the lottery ticket first — it costs one console command.**
`Holdable.AttachBone` is a `config name` defaulting to `"R_Grip"`, so the
existing `EngineExec` `set` channel can try:

```
set Pistol AttachBone L_Grip
```

If the rig has a left grip bone, the weapon changes hands using the game's own
attachment system and this session mostly evaporates. **Caveat: `R_Grip` is the
only such name anywhere in the corpus**, so there is no positive evidence
`L_Grip` exists.

Otherwise, three routes, cheapest first:

1. **Swap which controller drives the actor** — nearly free. `aimSource` has no
   left option today. The weapon then lives in your left hand while the model
   still renders as a right hand holding it; most VR mods accept exactly this.
2. **Mirror with `DrawScale3D` negative Y** (`+0x2B4`) — one write, and it will
   almost certainly render inside-out because a negative scale flips triangle
   winding. Five-minute experiment, expect it to fail.
3. **Swap the clusters properly** with S1's mechanism — the correct answer, and
   it composes with the other two features.

## Do not
Scale bone 43 · write clusters while the M7 motion signal is being trusted ·
gate any of this on `CutsceneTheater` · assume the array is model-space without
the measurement in S1.
